# DA, DR and Traffic: The Three Numbers on Every Link Listing, in Plain Words

Open any link seller's catalogue and three numbers stare back at you: DA, DR and traffic. They are not interchangeable, they do not come from Google, and using the wrong one for the wrong job is how buyers overpay. Here they are, one at a time, without the jargon.

**DA — Domain Authority (Moz).** A 0–100 score guessing how strong a whole domain is, based mainly on the links pointing at it. Useful for rough comparisons between domains in the same list. Weakness: it is Moz's opinion, computed from Moz's index — a domain can look strong here and ordinary elsewhere.

**DR — Domain Rating (Ahrefs).** Also 0–100, also about the domain's link profile, but built differently: DR cares a lot about how many different sites link in, and how strong those sites are. Guest post sellers usually price by DR tiers — DR 30+, DR 50+, DR 70+ — because buyers of editorial placements think in Ahrefs terms.

**Traffic — estimated visitors.** The odd one out: it measures people, not links. A site with real search traffic is almost certainly a real site, which makes traffic a lie detector rather than a ranking metric. A "DR 60" blog with zero traffic for two years deserves a question; a DR 35 blog with steady visitors is probably exactly what it claims to be.

**How to use them together.** Shortlist by the score the seller prices on (usually DR for guest posts, DA for PBN domains), then sanity-check with traffic and a look at the site itself. Never compare a DA from one catalogue with a DR from another — they are different rulers.
